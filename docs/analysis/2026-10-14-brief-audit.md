# AEA onboarding brief legal ethical and engineering audit

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-14, America/Chicago. **Status:** research complete for this pass;
recommendations await Innocent's decisions. This document owns T1–T23 and the internal
consistency review C1–C11. T1–T13 retain the IDs
from the [original hypotheses](../../../Gauntlet%20AI/artifacts/2026-10-13-partner-projects-todo.md).
Source IDs and review dates belong to the [source register](../source/source-register.md).
Unresolved questions belong to [research questions](research-questions.md).

The business problem is avoidable abandonment during sensitive-service registration: repeated
entry, uncertainty and friction waste acquisition spend and weaken trust. The defensible goal is
more **voluntary, accurately completed administrative intake**, with confirmed next steps and
less effort. Completion bought through pressure, unnecessary data collection or invented
assessments would undermine that value.

The [issued brief](../source/aea-proj1-onboarding-assistant.md) contains concrete vendor and
measurement pitfalls, plus gaps that could lead to unsafe implementation. It does **not** establish
that AEA intends to deceive candidates or requests an illegal product. Several original suspicions
were overstated. In particular, the brief expressly requires synthetic test IDs, consent, manual
fallbacks, confirmation of OCR fields, private storage and PII-free logs. Those are safeguards to
retain. No implementation exists yet, so the findings below concern requirements and proposed
interpretations, not demonstrated application vulnerabilities.

## Scope and evidence

The brief is general sensitive-service onboarding. Administrative patient intake is our research
niche, not an imposed healthcare requirement or an approved deployment. The demo remains
synthetic under the repo's existing rule. A public demo can still receive real information if it
accepts unrestricted uploads or chat; a warning alone cannot enforce that boundary.

This pass examines US deployment risks and selected EU definitions that the original hypotheses
invoked. Applicable states, countries, legal roles, patient populations and contracts remain
unconfirmed. HIPAA depends on the actual entity relationship and information, not merely the word
healthcare. HHS explains that cloud providers handling ePHI for covered entities or their business
associates generally require a business associate agreement (BAA), even for encrypted storage
they cannot read. [HHS cloud guidance, S-39](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html).

**Reading the register:** “confirmed” means the brief text or cited source establishes the
specific gap or restriction. “Conditional” means exposure depends on use or jurisdiction.
“Narrowed” corrects an earlier hypothesis. High priority means resolve before the demo or before
the stated real-data scenario; medium means measurement, scope or optional-feature risk. Proposed
responses are research recommendations, not an approved PRD. Evidence listed in the last column
is still to be produced.

## Decisions and evidence that remain

The real guideline is conditional law plus explicit engineering boundaries: determine which
entities handle what data; obey each selected vendor's terms; collect only what is needed;
maintain truthful consent/deletion promises; keep clinical decisions and permissions outside
model authority; preserve voluntary exit; and measure actual outcomes. NIST and OWASP are
guidance, W3C supplies accessibility criteria, PCI DSS is an industry standard, vendor terms are
contractual conditions, and statutes/regulations supply legal duties when applicable. None of
those categories should be presented as interchangeable certification.

Recommendations needing Innocent's later decision are the administrative-only assessment,
enforced synthetic/adult demo scope, vendor/data boundary, crisis handling, omission of sentiment
profiling, fictional document format, and deletion/measurement definitions. Every T row remains
open for mitigation or a reasoned disposition; “source verified” does not mean “risk fixed.”

Most recommendations clarify or strengthen the brief and preserve all four core flows. Potential
departures needing a recorded explanation are fixture-format expectations (T8), honest limits
on deletion for a real care workflow (T10), and any interpretation of complete fields that forbids
explicit unknown/incomplete states (T22). Raise a concrete grading conflict with staff if it
arises. No staff permission to deviate is established by this audit.

Innocent's approval gates remain open; this document does not authorize a PRD, design or build.
