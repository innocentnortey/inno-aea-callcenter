# American Oncology Network — Fourth Core Platform

**Role in this project:** the fourth core platform. Administrative intake is clearly relevant.
Referral-driven entry, clinical urgency and treatment scheduling make it a poor first demo.
**Practice example:** Florida Oncology & Hematology (FOH), described as "a division of American
Oncology Partners" on its forms (S-98). Its facts are labelled **practice** below and are not
generalised to the AON network.
**Sources read:** 2026-10-14 (S-20, S-21); re-read and extended **2026-10-15** (S-21, S-92…S-99).

## 1. Verified public facts

### 1.1 Platform level (aoncology.com)
- **What it is** (S-92): an alliance launched in 2018. It provides physician practices with a
  centralised specialty pharmacy, pathology, diagnostics, "fully integrated electronic medical
  records", care coordination and financial-assistance programmes.
- **Technology** (S-92): OncoEMR as the electronic medical record; a patient portal for messaging
  the care team; browser-based telehealth; quality reporting.
- **Network size:** the locations page (S-93) is paged and map-driven. The full count of practices
  and sites was **not established** in this pass. Practices seen include Bay Hematology Oncology,
  Cancer and Blood Specialists of Georgia, Central Georgia Cancer Care, Conway Hematology Oncology,
  Desert Hematology Oncology, Fort Wayne Medical Oncology and Hematology, and FOH.

### 1.2 Practice example: Florida Oncology & Hematology
- **Offices** (S-21, S-96): Cape Coral, Clearwater, Fort Myers, Naples–Goodlette and Naples–Lintree.
  Each has a phone, fax and referral form. Office **hours were not found** on the pages read.
- **People** (S-94): 10 physicians and 15 advanced practitioners (APRN, NP, PA) are linked from the
  site.
- **Services** (S-94): medical oncology, hematology, BiTE therapy, diagnostic imaging, radioligand
  therapy, an on-site lab and pathology, a specialty pharmacy, care coordination and navigation,
  clinical trials, financial support, survivorship care and a free dietitian (S-97).
- **How a new patient starts** (S-95, S-96):
  - "You may become our patient through a physician's referral or transfer"; second opinions are
    welcome.
  - For new-patient appointments, "consult your primary care provider or specialty provider for a
    referral or call (239) 217-8011."
  - Referring providers use per-office referral forms; "a Patient Services Specialist will contact
    you".
  - The contact form cannot schedule: "please call our office or use the patient portal". The
    patient portal is CareSpace.
  - The contact form asks patients **not** to send SSNs, medical details or card numbers through it.
- **Stated timing:** "we strive to keep waiting times for appointments to a minimum" (S-95). No
  number is given.
- **What to bring** (S-95): a medication list, prior illnesses, previous surgeries, allergies and
  the insurance card. Bringing a family member or friend is suggested.
- **New-patient packet** (S-98, 14 pages, file dated 2025-11-05). Administrative parts:
  - consent to treatment;
  - demographics, including an SS# field, preferred language, ethnicity and race, contact and
    voicemail permissions, and pharmacy;
  - primary care and referring physicians, and an emergency contact;
  - an advance-directives question;
  - a photograph authorisation and a request for release of records;
  - a disclosure consent;
  - insurance, including the policy-holder's SS# and prescription coverage;
  - a privacy-notice acknowledgment and financial policies (copays due at the time of service,
    assignment of benefits);
  - an SMS/email consent and a patient-assistance authorisation.

  Clinical parts (medical history, medications, review of systems) are out of scope for the product.
- **Money** (S-95, S-97): "We accept most major insurance plans" (no list published). A financial
  counsellor meets the patient "during one of your initial visits". Online payment
  (personapay.com) and payment plans are available.

## 2. Our interpretation (inference)

- **Entry runs mostly through referral or transfer, and staff coordinate it.** An intake product's
  role is limited to registration, the records-release and document checklist, consents and
  communication preferences, and help content. Urgency and clinical routing belong to clinicians.
- **The packet is long and paper-shaped.** Fourteen pages carry many administrative fields that
  could be collected once, digitally, before the first visit.
- **Records from the previous provider look like a real prerequisite.** Both the packet and one
  public review point to it. Inference to confirm (RQ-11).
- **CareSpace is a published portal, not evidence about the new-patient packet.** Whether the
  packet can be completed in it before the first visit is unknown (RQ-42). The two serious Naples
  reviews concern reaching new-patient scheduling and a transferred patient's follow-up; the
  results follow-up part is clinical and out of scope
  ([review report §7](../2026-10-15-google-review-sentiment.md#7-patient-status-and-journey-stage-of-the-cited-complaints)).

## 3. What we must not assume

- That other AON practices use FOH's packet, process or portal (RQ-9-style variation, RQ-11).
- That a patient can self-schedule a first oncology visit (RQ-8).
- That the product may judge urgency or route clinically. It may not (source-authority rule 7).
- That SSN is required because two packet fields ask for it (RQ-15).
