## 1. Verified public facts

### 1.1 Footprint, services and people
- **Offices:** 33 in 7 states on 2026-10-15 (S-69): Kentucky 14, Indiana 6, South Carolina 5,
  Tennessee 4, Ohio 2, Illinois 1, North Carolina 1. The homepage says "32+ locations" (S-64) and
  the About page "more than 30 clinics" (S-65). *The 2026-10-14 "20 cities in 3 states" reading is
  superseded.*
- **Hours are published per office** (S-69, S-70). Example: Louisville South (4420 Dixie Hwy; the
  office Google labels "Shively") is open Mon–Fri 7:45 AM–5:00 PM by appointment and closed
  weekends. On the day read, 11 of 33 offices were marked "By Appointment Only".
- **Services** (S-76): injections (epidural, facet, joint, SI, trigger point and others), nerve
  blocks, nerve ablation, neurostimulation, kyphoplasty, MILD, Intracept, Botox, medication
  management, psychological pain assessment, chronic care management, occupational medicine,
  clinical research. **Complete Care** (S-66) adds Remote Therapeutic Monitoring, Behavioral Health
  Integration, a patient portal and a "24/7 Virtual Agent".
- **Providers:**
  - The directory (S-71) has 17 pages, each provider listed with credential and offices. It says
    "We accept nearly all insurance and are accepting new patients at all our locations." Full
    enumeration was not completed (the pager was not scriptable).
  - Referral forms map physicians to offices (S-74; versions differ — the 2025-03-06 form lists 20
    physicians and 1 physical therapist).
  - The new-patient letter lists physicians as board certified in pain medicine and anesthesiology
    (S-72).
  - Office pages list their own providers. Louisville South lists 2 MDs and 1 APRN (S-70).

### 1.2 How a new patient starts (published)
- **Online request form** (S-75; viewed, nothing entered), "Powered by" prettygoodai. Required
  fields:
  - name, phone, email, date of birth;
  - preferred clinic, and whether the patient has been seen before;
  - previous or referring provider, chosen by search or typed manually ("We'll request your
    records so your visit goes smoothly");
  - full address and gender;
  - insurance company (from a list) and member ID;
  - reason for referral and preferred contact method;
  - whether the pain relates to a motor-vehicle accident or a work injury.

  **It asks for no SSN and no ID or card upload.**
- **Published path** (S-70): request → "We'll Connect With Your Physician" → "Once your
  physician's referral is received, we'll reach out to schedule your initial appointment."
- **Other channels:** call (502) 855-7200 (FAQ, S-12) or new-patient scheduling 502-890-5639 /
  newpatients@mypainsolution.com (S-11); office phone numbers (S-69). Referring providers fax
  (866) 697-2464 (S-11, S-74). Existing patients are pointed to the athenahealth patient portal
  (S-64, S-66); bill pay is through athenahealth (S-12). Records requests go through ChartRequest
  (link on S-64).
- **What a "complete referral" contains** (S-11): demographics ("Name, Address, SSN, etc."),
  insurance cards, recent notes and imaging. **This describes the referring provider's fax packet.
  It is not a list of fields the patient must enter.** The first pass presented SSN as a patient
  requirement; that was wrong.

### 1.3 Referral, insurance and money (published)
- **Referral wording conflicts.** The FAQ's General section says "most insurance plans require a
  referral" and that the scheduling team obtains it from the PCP. Its Insurance section says "Some
  insurance plans require a referral … Others allow you to schedule directly" (S-12, S-67). The
  plan list marks specific plans "Referral required", e.g. UnitedHealthcare Medicare (S-67).
- **Accepted plans** are listed by state, with notes such as out-of-network only for Devoted
  Health (S-67). The request form offers a shorter carrier list (S-75).
- **Self-pay:** "A Self-Pay option is available to accommodate uninsured patients only" (S-12).
  Uninsured patients are told to call before the appointment, and the practice offers information
  about expected costs (S-12, S-67).
- **Fees and payment:**
  - "Cancellations with less than 24-hour notice are subject to a $50.00 fine"; arriving more than
    10 minutes late may mean rescheduling (S-12).
  - Fees are due at the time of service (S-72).
  - Payment plans are available, and cost estimates are offered "based on your insurance
    benefits" (S-12).
- **Prior authorisation:** "Some only take a few days, while others can take weeks" (S-12).
  Motor-vehicle cases are seen once PIP (personal injury protection) is exhausted. Workers'
  compensation needs authorisation before scheduling (S-12).

### 1.4 Stated timing commitments (published response aims, not measurements)
- "Our new patient scheduling team strives to schedule all patients within 10 days of referral or
  initial contact" (S-12).
- "Our scheduling staff is devoted to finding appointments for new patients within ten business
  days of initial contact" (S-12); the patient guarantee (S-68) and new-patient letter (S-72) say
  "within ten business days of referral or initial contact".
- "We aim to get you seen within two weeks" once the physician's referral is received (S-70).

These are three phrasings of one aim, and they are not identical: 10 days, ten business days, and
two weeks after the referral arrives.

### 1.5 Preparation and arrival instructions (published; not response times)
- New patients: arrive 30 minutes early if the forms are not completed in advance. Follow-ups:
  15 minutes early. Procedures: 30 minutes early (S-12).
- Bring insurance card(s), a valid unexpired photo ID (accepted types are listed on S-11), a method
  of co-payment, and recent imaging or consultations (S-12).
- For procedures: an empty stomach; a driver if sedated; blood thinners stopped only with the
  treating physician's approval (S-12). *These are clinical instructions the company publishes.
  The product would only display them as approved content.*
- The FAQ says new-patient forms can be downloaded. No new-patient form PDF was found among the
  links on the pages read (S-11, S-12). The registration page offers the new-patient letter and
  privacy notice.

### 1.6 Positioning (published claims)
- Homepage (S-64): "Led by Dual Board-Certified Physicians", "Care That's Built Around You",
  "Advanced, Minimally Invasive Pain Treatments", and "Convenient Access, When You Need It … prompt
  scheduling".
- About page (S-65): the mission of "quickly responding with compassionate, personalized, and
  comprehensive treatment plans", and a Responsiveness value ("From timely access to care to
  persistent follow-up").
- Guarantee (S-68): "We will accept all new patients … We will not 'cherry pick'".

## 2. Our interpretation (inference — not company statements)

- **It is still the strongest match to the brief.** Intake involves documents (a photo ID,
  insurance cards and a referral packet). Prerequisites are coordinated by staff (referral,
  coverage, prior authorisation). There is a published speed promise and a patient population
  living with chronic pain.
- **Digital entry tools already exist.** The request form (contact and referral capture), the
  virtual agent (described for scheduling needs and connecting to the care team), the portal
  (existing patients) and records requests are all published. They establish that digital tools
  are present. They do **not** establish complete digital onboarding, successful integration
  between them, or failure. The product must be framed as complementing those tools, not as the
  company's first digital intake.
- **Most cited low reviews concern established patients or existing appointments.** The clearest
  new-patient reports are a referred patient who waited for a call and an older report of a
  six-week referral-to-first-visit interval ([review report §7](../2026-10-15-google-review-sentiment.md#7-patient-status-and-journey-stage-of-the-cited-complaints)).
- **"Closing the seams between existing systems" is still a hypothesis.** Public reviews (S-77)
  report information re-entered several times, a booking confirmed by an automated assistant but
  not found at check-in, and trouble reaching a person. They do not establish which system is
  involved, the cause, or the frequency (RQ-28, RQ-38).
- **The company makes measurable promises** (a scheduling aim of about ten days, "prompt
  scheduling", "persistent follow-up"). Those promises are what an onboarding product can support
  and measure.

## 3. What we must not assume

- That the request form, the "24/7 Virtual Agent" and the automated phone system reviewers
  describe are the same product or vendor (RQ-28).
- That the ten-day aim is met or missed; no data is public (RQ-2, RQ-38).
- That a self-booked first visit is allowed without the referral and coverage steps (RQ-7, RQ-8).
- That SSN or ID upload is required from patients online; the published form asks for neither
  (RQ-6).
- That one office's hours, providers or reviews describe all 33 offices.
- Anything clinical. An intake summary is not triage, diagnosis or insurance approval.
