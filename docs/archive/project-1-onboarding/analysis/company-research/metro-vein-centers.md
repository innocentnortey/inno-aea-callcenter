# Metro Vein Centers — Reuse and Benchmarking Candidate

**Role in this project:** a reuse and benchmarking candidate. It already publishes a short
free-evaluation form, a chatbot (per its privacy policy), an insurance-specialist process and SMS
reminders. **It is not a pitch to introduce digital intake.** Nothing reviewed shows that its
current tools perform poorly.
**Sources read:** 2026-10-14 (S-17…S-19); re-read and extended **2026-10-15** (S-18, S-19,
S-85…S-90). See the [source register](../../source/source-register.md).

## 1. Verified public facts

### 1.1 Footprint, services and people
- **Clinics:** 80 in 9 states (S-89): TX 24, NY 17, NJ 12, MI 8, AZ 7, CT 6, IL 4, GA 1, PA 1.
- **Treatments** (S-86): sclerotherapy, radiofrequency ablation, microphlebectomy, excel V+ laser,
  endovenous laser ablation, Varithena and VenaSeal, plus compression after most treatments. The
  first visit includes an evaluation and an ultrasound (S-88).
- **Clinicians** (S-86): the team includes "physicians, vascular surgeons, general surgeons, nurse
  practitioners and registered vascular ultrasound technicians". The FAQ says all doctors are
  board-certified and certified by the American Board of Venous and Lymphatic Medicine.
- **Practice example, Forest Hills** (S-90): two named physicians; open Mon 9:30–7:00, Tue 8–5,
  Wed 8–4, Thu 8–5, Fri 8–4:30, closed weekends.
- **Spanish-language site:** the site links an `/es` version (S-85 links).

### 1.2 How a new patient starts (published)
- **"Book Your Free Evaluation" form** (S-88; viewed only): first name, last name, email and phone
  are required, plus a newsletter opt-in. The page says "We'll contact you shortly to schedule an
  evaluation." Phone booking is offered too.
- **The free evaluation is "subject to insurance eligibility"** (S-88).
- **Chatbot** (S-19): handles "appointment requests, insurance verification, and general
  inquiries" and stores data in HubSpot.
- **Insurance process, six published steps** (S-87):
  1. Verification on the first call.
  2. The first visit is "automatically quoted prior to your visit … as long as you provide your
     insurance information at time of scheduling".
  3. Pre-authorisation after the consultation, if treatment is deemed necessary.
  4. An estimate on request, with "a specialist will call you within 24-48 business hours".
  5. If approved, treatment goes ahead; if not, financing options are reviewed.
  6. Claims are handled after treatment.

  "Insurance verification takes about 24–48 hours" after the form is submitted.
- **Rescheduling** (S-86): call 866-715-9121 or "text us back at the same number you receive
  appointment reminders from". SMS reminders are sent to opted-in patients (S-19).
- **Records:** requested by email (S-86).
- **Published care sequence** (S-121, read 2026-10-15): initial consultation → ultrasound → clinician-created treatment plan → insurance coordination → treatment appointments. This describes what the company publishes, not observed internal handoffs or guaranteed coverage.

### 1.3 Insurance and money (published)
- 200+ plans; Medicare and "Medicaid* … *accepted in select states"; a top-plans list (S-87).
- "91% of our vein treatments are covered by insurance" (S-86, S-88). This is the company's claim.
- Treatment judged purely cosmetic "costs $425 per session"; self-pay treatment "can range from $425
  to $1,500" (S-86).
- Self-pay and financing are available; checks, debit and credit cards and NFC mobile payments are
  accepted (S-87, S-88).

### 1.4 Positioning (published claims)
- "We treat people, not symptoms."; patient-centred care; board-certified specialists; innovation
  (S-85).
- "98% Satisfaction Rating", "Over 20,000 5-Star reviews" and "helped over 200,000 patients" (S-88).
  These are company claims, not verified.
- Insurance specialist: "A real person who answers your insurance questions. No chat bots, no
  guesswork" (S-87). This sits uneasily with the privacy policy's description of a chatbot for
  insurance verification (S-19). It may mean the specialist step is human while the first contact
  is a chatbot. That is an inference.

## 2. Our interpretation (inference)

- **The closest public benchmark for a light-touch request.** A four-field request is followed by a
  call, verification within 24–48 hours, and a quote before the first visit.
- **ID OCR has no published purpose at the request stage**, which asks for no ID or date of birth.
  The insurance card matters more here.
- **The likely friction is after the request.** Reviews (S-91) report appointment times changed
  without notice, day-before cancellations, missing confirmations and directions, and a language
  request not met. This is an attributed-experience hypothesis at one clinic (Forest Hills). The
  causes (scheduling capacity, staffing or messaging) are not established, and an intake product
  cannot by itself fix clinic-side schedule changes.

## 3. What we must not assume

- That the chatbot or the form underperforms (RQ-13).
- That HubSpot is the scheduling system of record (RQ-18).
- That 80 clinics are 80 deployments; they are one AEA platform.
- That "free" means free for every patient; the site says "subject to insurance eligibility".
