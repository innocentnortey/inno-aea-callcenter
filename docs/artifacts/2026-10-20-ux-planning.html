The story so far

AEA wants Meridian’s call center to expand call center coverage without adding headcount by deflecting a majority of calls to an AI agent. Meridian is a made-up Austin heating, cooling and plumbing company.

So we are building a chat agent for Meridian’s homeowners. It answers the routine questions from the current help files. It books visits. It gives the safety instruction first when someone is at risk. Everything else goes to a person, honestly: a reference number and a promise it can keep.

Who uses it Fact

  • Homeowner: uses the chat.
  • Representative: picks up a handoff.
  • Supervisor: makes sure nothing urgent or late is left.
  • Business owner: asks if the AI cuts load without hurting customers.
  • Reviewer: AEA’s grader, who will try to break it and watch the agent escalate.

PRD §2; USER_STORIES.md. In the demo, the reviewer signs in once with the demo login and plays every role through a one-click, labelled role switch (OD-1, OD-2, R-48).

What Stages 1 to 3 settled Your decision

  • Stage 1: the PRD 0.2 scope and targets (“yes to all”, 2026-10-20).
  • HP-1: your two-layer design. Code guards run first; a planner picks tasks; a responder with no tools writes the words.
  • HP-2: rules first for danger; a small classifier may only raise the risk.
  • HP-3: the AI never makes a change that carries the $49 fee.
  • HP-4: a completion check and a fact check on every reply.

PRD v0.4 header; hard-problems.md (all four chosen, Innocent ✓ 2026-10-20).

Two words you’ll see a lot. A flow is the path one person takes to finish one job. A contract (CON) is the request a screen sends to the backend, like an order slip passed to the kitchen. Most customer flows happen on one screen, the chat. Every message and every button on a reply card goes through the same contract, CON-2.

Journeys: one per user type

A journey follows one person from start to finish. The line above each journey is how we aim for them to feel at each step, from 1 (bad) to 5 (good). The low points are on purpose: they are the moments the design has to carry, like a gas smell or a surprise fee.

Why the contact offer waits (UX-2 finding R2-2, 2026-10-20) Proposal

Meridian’s policy says to leave “immediately” and call “from outside”. The gas utility’s own page says not to use a phone indoors. So the card is Meridian’s instruction word for word, with no field, no question and no prompt. The optional offer (R-6, WP-5) is one line meant for after they are outside. This changes when the offer appears, not the requirement.

FLOW-5 · Book a repair, tune-up or estimate Proposal: draft flow

Booking is where the AI changes real data. First the customer sees open windows and the standard fee for their ZIP or city, and nothing private is asked. The code comes just before the booking is written. Then the member fee applies if they are a member. The slot is checked again at the moment of writing, so two people can never get the same window.

Why the code comes after the windows (UX-2 finding R1-1, 2026-10-20) Proposal

A real Austin HVAC booking widget asked for name, phone and email before showing any time. Showing the arrival windows and the standard fee first costs nothing private: the zone comes from a ZIP or city the customer types. A booking is an account change, so the code still comes before the write (R-15; consolidated §8 step 2). The member fee and member-reserved windows need the verified record, so they appear after the code.

Sitemap: one login, three surfaces, two report pages, twelve screens

The product is one app. Nothing answers without the demo login (SCR-12), except a health check. Behind it are three surfaces and two read-only report pages. The chat is the default. The demo tools exist so a grader can see what is simulated. Staff screens have their own header and look. A role switch (SCR-4) sits in every header, so one click moves the reviewer between customer and staff, with no second password. The two reports, monitoring and evaluation, hold only redacted totals and open in any role. The chat is built phone first; the staff console laptop first, still usable on a phone.
