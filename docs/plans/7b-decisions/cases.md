## For Innocent

### CAS-16: what priority does a burning smell get once both dangers are ruled out?

**The story.** WP-6 is the burning-smell chat: *"I need to schedule a service call. I smell something burning from the
vents."* The AI asks whether a breaker has already tripped and whether there is smoke, sparks or flames. There are three
paths, and you checked all three on 2026-10-20. "Yes" to either is the P0 safety path. "I'm not sure" is the cautious
path: a conditional safety instruction, the emergency number and **a P1 case**. "Clearly no to both" gets *"the earliest
repair booking, marked urgent, plus a priority case"* (WP-6 step 3; PRD R-7; consolidated §9.2 step 4).

Every source says "a priority case", and none says which priority. In this design a priority is a specific level from
P0 to P4, set by code, never by the model. The level decides where the case sits in the staff queue, whether the
supervisor is alerted at once, and which supervisor lane it appears in. P0 and P1 sit above everything and alert at once.
P1 is defined as "a burning smell **with unclear risk**". This case is the one where the risk has been ruled out. So the
level is a judgment about how much risk to carry on a smell the customer says is not dangerous, and code can't pick it.

It is cheap to change now: one row in the priority table (`policy/handoff.py`, AEA-14) and one new reason value
(AEA-02). After the build it is the same row plus its tests. The graph topic's AEA-36 item 2 asks the same question for
the routing side; one answer serves both.

**Option A: P2 time-sensitive, new reason `burning_ruled_out` (recommended).** The case sorts above every routine and
standard case, after P0/P1. Its internal due time is the urgent booking's window start, so staff are asked to look
before the technician arrives (the same pattern you approved for fee-bearing changes, R-21). If no slot could be booked,
the due time is the standard promise due. The customer sees the standard promise ("within 1 business day") and the
booking. *Gain:* staff see it before the visit, and it does not count as an emergency, so M-17 (false alarms) stays
honest. *Give up:* no immediate supervisor alert.

**Option B: P1, the same as "unclear".** Every burning smell is emergency-priority, whatever the answers. *Gain:* the
most cautious choice, and the simplest rule. *Give up:* it erases the distinction WP-6 draws between "unclear" and "ruled
out", adds a P1 alert for a case the customer says is safe, and inflates M-17.

**Option C: P3 standard, reason `burning_ruled_out`.** It is treated like any case that policy routes to a person.
*Gain:* the fewest alerts. *Give up:* the "urgent" booking and the case point in different directions, and staff could
reach it after the visit.

*Recommended: A.* It is the only option where the case's level matches the booking's "urgent" label without calling a
ruled-out smell an emergency.

> **Decision needed:** For a burning smell where the customer clearly rules out a tripped breaker and smoke, sparks or
> flames, which priority should the case carry: P2 with its due time at the booked window (A), P1 (B) or P3 (C)?
